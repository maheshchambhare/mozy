In short

Eight things that decide whether this works

1

Small groups first, the world second

People try much harder against 10 rivals than 10,000. In STEP UP, groups of 3 with a weekly leaderboard walked 920 more steps a day. Crews and family are the heart; country and world are the backdrop.

2

Show the gap, not just the rank

"312 steps to pass papa" beats "#2". On big boards show "top 18%", never a bare "#48,201"; percentiles are also what people share most.

3

Open the iPhone door

Today an invited iPhone friend lands on a paywall they can't close. Unless the race works without paying, every iPhone invite dies there (D6).

4

Every invite works without the app

A short link to a web page, WhatsApp first, QR on story cards. Android carries the code through the install; iPhone gets a paste button. No paid link service.

5

A name and one number per day

Only the race name, pet look, optional country and one daily total leave the phone, after an explicit yes. Re-login comes from a secure backup of a random token, not a device ID.

6

Rules that come with it

A 13+ rating, report and block, in-app deletion, new privacy labels, parental approval for minors in some places, and no marketing use of anyone's steps.

7

$0 to start, $5 a month at launch

Cloudflare's Free plan covers the beta; Workers Paid ($5) removes the daily cliff and lasts to roughly 30,000–50,000 daily racers. About $11 a month at 100,000.

8

Rivals are already racing

Stroll added global and country step boards in September; Lockt runs leagues with vouchers; WeWard now locks apps until a step goal. A leaderboard alone won't stand out. Mozy's edge is families, the pet, fairness and privacy.

The growth loop

How one walk turns into a new user

The race is worth building only if it pulls new people in. Every step of this loop must work without paid ads and without the friend having Mozy yet.

Steps 5–6 are where most loops leak. On iPhone today an invited friend hits a paywall they can't close (decision D6), and iPhones can't carry an invite code through the App Store install, so the code must be pasted.

Research

What the evidence says

1 · Why competition works, and when it backfires

Findings from social psychology, behavioural economics and step-count trials, ranked by how much they should shape Mozy Race. The first five decide its structure.

STEP UP trial (602 adults, 24 weeks)Extra steps a day vs control12 weeks after it ended
Competition: groups of 3, weekly leaderboard+920+569
Support+689+428
Collaboration+637+126 (not significant)

Patel et al., JAMA Internal Medicine, 2019. Competition was the only arm that clearly lasted.

1 · small groups first

Fewer rivals, more effort

Told they faced 10 rivals, people finished a quiz in 28.95 s; told 100, in 33.15 s. Motivation to compete was 4.07 vs 2.00 (of 7) for 10 vs 10,000 rivals. Rank in a small local group outweighs standing in a big pool.

Mozy: crews, family and leagues are home; the world board is the backdrop. Garcia & Tor 2009; Zell & Alicke 2010

2 · compare, don't just cheer

Comparison beats support

Students in groups of 6 who could compare themselves went to ~90% more exercise classes than groups who couldn't; support-only groups did worst (16.8 vs 20.3 for control).

Mozy: ranked weekly groups are the engine; reactions are extras. Zhang et al. 2016; STEP UP 2019

3 · keep rivals reachable

Match people on ability

A star whose success looks reachable inspires; an unreachable one deflates. In 26 countries (103,558 students), people surrounded by stronger peers rated themselves lower.

Mozy: leagues by 2–4-week step average and time zone. Lockwood & Kunda 1997; Marsh & Hau 2003

4 · show the gap

Effort rises near the goal

Café customers bought coffee ~20% more often as a free one got closer; a 12-stamp card with 2 stamps pre-filled was finished faster (12.7 days) than a plain 10-stamp card (15.6).

Mozy: ±3 ranks and "418 steps to pass riya". Kivetz, Urminsky & Zheng 2006

5 · lines near everyone

Competition peaks at a cut-off

Competitiveness was 6.05 at #100 vs #101 (a bonus line) but 4.29 at #50 vs #51. Effort is U-shaped: people try hardest after finishing first or last.

Mozy: promotion and drop zones, a top 3 in every group, daily crowns. Garcia, Tor & Gonzalez 2006; Gill et al. 2019

6 · fresh starts

Reset on Mondays

Among 11,912 students, gym visits rose 33.4% at the start of a week, 14.4% at the start of a month and 47.1% at the start of a semester.

Mozy: Monday resets, monthly awards, festival weeks, New Year. Dai, Milkman & Riis 2014

7 · teams and families

Compete together, against others

Family game BE FIT: goals met on 53% vs 32% of days, +953 steps a day, still +494 after it ended. A meta-analysis found competition combined with collaboration worked best for behaviour.

Mozy: family crews with a shared goal, crew-vs-crew weeks. Patel 2017; Sailer & Homner 2020

8 · loss framing, sparingly

"You'll lose it" works, pressure doesn't

Rewards framed as something to lose: goal met on 45% of days vs 30% (control); gain or lottery framing changed nothing. Pressure weakens people's own wish to take part.

Mozy: at most one "you were passed" alert a day, always with the gap. Patel 2016; Reeve & Deci 1996

9 · streaks need repair

A broken streak is the danger

93% carried on after an intact streak, 69% after a broken one, 85% when it could be repaired. In a 61,293-person megastudy, the best idea rewarded coming back after a miss.

Mozy: streak freezes earned by walking, a "welcome back" crown. Silverman & Barasch 2022; Milkman 2021

10 · protect the bottom

Nobody wants last place

People dislike last place more than any other rank; losing lowers drive by making people feel less capable. Fitness is where users rate leaderboards most favourably.

Mozy: percentiles on big boards, "most improved", an option to hide your rank, never a "last" label. Kuziemko 2014; Reeve & Deci 1996; Jia et al., CHI 2017

11 · score healthy behaviour

Cap what shouldn't be maxed

Duolingo changed league points to reward the lessons that teach best. Tracking an activity makes people do more of it but enjoy it less.

Mozy: leagues count steps up to a daily cap (D16). Duolingo 2024; Etkin 2016

12 · effects fade

Plan seasons, not a one-off

Gamification's effect on activity fell from g=0.42 to 0.15 at follow-up; one review called the average +489 steps a day "trivial"; only 8% of 54 gym interventions kept working after they ended.

Mozy: seasons, re-matching, events; never promise health outcomes. Mazeas 2022; Nishi 2024; Milkman 2021

Gen Z: movement is social life

  • Strava's Year in Sport 2025 (30,000+ people): Gen Z were 75% more likely to name a race or event as their main motivation, and 39% more likely than Gen X to use fitness to meet people. Clubs grew about 4× in 2025.
  • Its headline: "Doomscrolling Is Out, Movement Is In", which is Mozy's whole pitch.
  • 55% of Gen Z say social connection is their top reason to join a fitness group; twice as many Gen Z as Gen X find starting a new sport intimidating, so a gentle "rookie week" helps.

Duolingo leagues, for reference

  • Tested in 2018, modelled on FarmVille 2's leagues; now 10 tiers; a new league each week, matched on similar habits and time zone; private profiles can opt out.
  • Its S-1 filing says leaderboards raised learning time by "almost 20%"; its former product chief reported +17% and three times as many highly engaged learners.
  • League size (~30) and promotion counts come from third-party guides only (unverified).

Mozy's direct rivals are already racing

  • Stroll (steps to screen time) added global and country boards for day, week and month on 21 Sep 2026.
  • Lockt (walk to unlock) added monthly leagues with Amazon vouchers on 28 Jun 2026.
  • WalkLock and Steppin have friend challenges.
  • WeWard's Walking Mode (iOS, Jul 2026) now locks apps until a step goal, Mozy's core idea.
  • So a leaderboard alone won't stand out. Mozy's edge has to be families, the pet, fairness and privacy.

What to copy

  • Weekly is the main clock (Apple, Duolingo, Strava clubs, STEP UP); daily is a side view, monthly is for awards.
  • Score families against their own goals (Apple's % of goal, Fitbit's Goal Day), so a grandmother can beat a college student (D17).
  • Leagues and percentiles, not one giant board (Duolingo, Samsung's top-10%/30% lines).
  • A beatable rival for everyone: StepUp uses bots; Mozy can use the pet itself, clearly labelled as your Mozy, never a fake person.
  • Private by default, global opt-in, minors gated (StepUp, Samsung, Stroll).
  • Friend streaks: Duolingo's Friend Streak made a daily lesson 22% more likely.
  • Ship only what you can keep running: Fitbit's removal of its community features angered users.

3 · What makes people share and invite

Rank > stat

Duolingo added the learner's XP percentile to its year-in-review card and share rates rose; the top 10% of learners made over half of all shares. "Top 6%!" and "Top 1%!" were the most shared posts.

For Mozy: percentile cards ("top 9% in india"), offered only to the top half.

India shares

In Duolingo's data, India and Brazil had below-average open rates but the highest share rates.

For Mozy: design for WhatsApp first: text grids, Status-sized cards, Hindi-friendly titles later.

Everyone gets one

Spotify Wrapped 2025: 200M+ engaged users in 24 hours and 500M+ shares. Duolingo's second card, "learner styles", raised sharing for people who weren't at the top.

For Mozy: identity labels worked out on the phone ("weekend warrior") so the bottom half has a card too.

90 → 2M

Wordle went from 90 players (1 Nov 2021) to 2M+ in early January 2022, after the spoiler-free emoji grid that players invented.

For Mozy: the week grid as copyable text: 🟩🟩🟨🟩⬜🟩🟩.

Fads fade

BeReal's daily window took it from 21.6M to 73.5M users in a month (2022), then daily users fell from about 15M to about 6M. Gas hit #1 and shut down within about a year.

For Mozy: novelty mechanics are experiments, not the core.

Never paywall it

Strava made Year in Sport subscribers-only in December 2025 and got a backlash.

For Mozy: race, cards and invites free for everyone, on both platforms.

Device ids: what the rules allow

  • Google calls ANDROID_ID a persistent device identifier that may not be linked to other personal data, so it must not be the account key or be stored next to names and steps.
  • iPhone's vendor id changes when all your apps are removed, and the app can't rely on it.
  • So "automatic re-login" comes from secure backups of a random token instead (see "How it's built").

Still to check (unverified)

  • Whether a names-and-ranks board counts as "social media" in Apple's questionnaire.

What every board shows

  • Today · week · month on every arena. All-time totals only as personal bests: an all-time board is unwinnable for anyone who joins late.
  • A hero card with your place and the gap to the next person.
  • Percentile on country and world boards. Below the median, lead with your own progress ("+2,140 on last week").
  • Top 3 + the people around you on big boards, so the next target is always reachable.
  • "synced 12 min ago" on every row and a resting tag, so nobody looks lazy because their phone was asleep.

Ways to win that aren't just "most steps"

  • Crowns 👑 for the day's and the week's top walker in each crew, worn by the pet until the next result.
  • Most improved against your own last 4 weeks (phase 2).
  • Most consistent: days at or above 8,000 steps (phase 2).
  • League move-ups every Monday (phase 2).
  • Rewards are cosmetic only: crowns, leaf colours, hats. Never minutes, never money.

Flows

How it feels, step by step

Joining the race (3 screens)

  1. Intro. "race your friends on steps" · crews, your country, the world · "Only your race name and one step total per day leave your phone."
  2. Profile. Race name (pre-filled from the onboarding name), pet colour, country flag (pre-filled from the phone's region, with "hide my flag").
  3. Who sees you. Two equal buttons, nothing pre-selected: "everyone" (world, country, crews, website) or "only my crews". Age confirmation and consent, then the boards.

Starting a crew

  1. Race → crews → "start a crew" → name, friends or family.
  2. The share sheet opens: "race me on mozy this week 🏁 I'm on 8,902 steps today. join sharma fam: mozy.mcrudra.com/j/SHRM7Q"
  3. "Show QR" for people in the same room.

  1. Every daythe daily loop

    Walk → the phone syncs → the rank moves → "papa passed you · 140 steps behind" (at most 2 race notifications a day, never 22:00–08:00) → walk → pass him → a card is offered once → share.

  2. Every weekthe weekly loop

    Sunday evening "final hours" (phase 2) → Monday 4 AM the week closes → crown to the winner and a result card → a fresh week for everyone, so last week's loser starts level.

  3. Every monthawards

  4. Any timeleaving

    Race profile → "delete my race profile" → everything on the server is deleted at once. Mozy itself keeps working exactly as before.

Hourly or per-walk steps, minutes earned or spent, which apps are blocked, Screen Time data, GPS, contacts. IP addresses are never stored. The wallet and the blocker stay fully offline, so a server outage can never lock or unlock anything.

How it's built

One small Worker, one database, everything else static

"No sign-up" accounts

Apps can't read a permanent hardware ID any more, and Google's rules say device IDs must not be tied to names or step data. So Mozy makes a random account token and keeps it in each platform's own secure backup. That gives the "it just knows me" feeling without a device ID.

  • iPhone: the token sits in the Keychain, synced by iCloud Keychain, so a reinstall or a new iPhone signs straight back in. Apple's DeviceCheck remembers "this phone had a race profile", so a fresh install offers restore instead of a duplicate.
  • Android: the token sits in Google's Block Store, which survives uninstalling (with backup on) and moves to a new phone; Auto Backup is a second layer.
  • Anyone else: a "Mozy ID" recovery code in the race profile.

The race day

A fixed 4 AM to 4 AM local day, the same as Mozy's default day, so for most people the race number equals the Home number. Night-owl walks at 1 AM count for "tonight".

  • Users who moved their reset hour (Settings offers 12–9 AM) get a separate race count, so nobody can stretch a day by changing the setting.
  • Week: Monday 4 AM. Month: the 1st, 4 AM.
  • Android uploads walked steps after the ride filter; iPhone uploads CMPedometer steps.

The whole upload

Sent on opening Mozy, then at most every 5 minutes while it's open and steps grew by 250; Android's step service syncs in the background at most every 30 minutes. One written row per change.

Ranks without big bills

  1. Every 10 minutes a Cron reads each live day once and builds every world and country board.
  2. Week and month boards add only the live days on top of a once-a-day total, never re-reading 30 days.
  3. Each board keeps the top 100 and a sorted list of all totals; your rank is a binary search, your percentile is 1 − (rank − 1) / n.
  4. Crew boards are tiny, so they're read live and cached for 60 seconds.

Notifications

  • Phase 1, local: the phone compares ranks after each sync. Android's step service runs all day, so it works there; on iPhone only when the app wakes.
  • Phase 2, push through Firebase Cloud Messaging (already in the app), which also delivers to iPhones.
  • At most 2 race notifications a day, none 22:00–08:00, each type can be turned off, never guilt copy.

Moderation without a team

  • Names: 2–16 characters, normalised, checked against blocklists (English, Hindi/Hinglish and other top languages) and reserved words; no links or handles.
  • Long-press any name to report; 3 different reporters hide it ("walker 4821") until reviewed.
  • Block a person; leave or remove from crews.
  • No chat, bios or photos. Phase 2 reactions are 5 fixed pet stickers inside crews.

Keeping it fair

  • Only Mozy's own sensor steps count. No HealthKit or Health Connect imports, so no manual entries; Android rides are already filtered out.
  • Phone shakers walk at walking pace (about 100–117 steps a minute), so rhythm limits won't catch them. Instead: a review line of 40,000 steps a day on public boards (corporate challenges cap at 20,000–50,000), a hard limit of 100,000, and a flag for minutes above ~250 steps.
  • Crews stay simple: friends see each other's real numbers; the review line only guards public boards.
  • Phase 2: Apple App Attest and Google Play Integrity once a day for anyone near the top of a public board (Play's default quota is 10,000 checks a day).
  • Review, don't accuse: flagged days stay visible to the person and their crews but wait before reaching public boards. Some "cheaters" are just people who trained hard. No prizes means little to gain.

When things go wrong

  • Any daily quota at 50% → alert; 70% → phones sync every 60 minutes; 85% → boards from snapshots only, phones keep totals and retry.
  • API down → blocking, earning and Home are untouched; the race screen shows the last boards it saw.
  • Daily totals kept 40 days, monthly totals 25 months; profiles with no sync for 13 months are deleted.

Your decisions

What I need from you before building

D1 · public boards

World and country boards: yes, with an explicit choice

You asked for them. Recommended: every racer picks "everyone" or "only my crews" at joining, with nothing pre-selected, and can change it any time.

D2 · country

Store a country code?

Recommended: yes, pre-filled from the phone's region setting (no location permission, no GPS), editable, and "hide my flag" allowed. It is the only new data point beyond your name-and-steps rule.

D3 · race day

4 AM to 4 AM local

Recommended over midnight: it matches Mozy's default day, so Home and the race show the same number for most people, and late-night walks count for "tonight". Alternative: midnight, like Apple and Fitbit.

D4 · names

Free-text race name with a filter

Recommended: the onboarding name pre-filled, 2–16 characters, blocklists, report and block. Alternative: generated names only ("sprouty walker 4821"), which need no moderation but feel less personal.

D5 · age

Minimum age

Recommended: 13+ to join the race, asked as birth month and year (a neutral screen). Public boards 16+. Under-13s keep Mozy fully local. In India, build a parental-consent path before 13 May 2027 or keep under-18s out of the race there. In Texas, Utah and Louisiana, minors need a parent's approval (Apple's PermissionKit) before the race turns on.

D6 · iPhone door

Let invited iPhone users race without paying

Today a non-subscriber can't get past the iPhone onboarding paywall, so every iPhone invite ends at a payment screen. The race only works if the paywall can be closed (with a free core) or skipped for people who arrive with an invite. This is the biggest single blocker.

D7 · Cloudflare plan

Free for beta, $5 at launch

Recommended: Workers Paid from public launch day, so a viral day can't switch the race off until midnight UTC.

D8 · push notifications

Local first, push in phase 2

Recommended: phase 1 uses local notifications (no new service); phase 2 adds Firebase Cloud Messaging, which needs a push token and a privacy-label line.

D9 · prizes

No prizes, cosmetics only

Recommended: crowns, leaf colours and hats for the pet. Prizes invite cheating and bring sweepstakes and tax rules.

D10 · names on the web

Public names on mozy.mcrudra.com?

Recommended: show public racers' race names and flags on the web boards, but keep name lists out of search engines (noindex) and index only totals and country stats.

D11 · how long data is kept

40 days of daily totals

Recommended: daily totals 40 days (enough for monthly boards and 4-week baselines; step histories are likely health data, so keep them short), monthly totals 25 months, profiles deleted after 13 months with no sync, immediate deletion on request.

D12 · names of things

Mozy Race, crews, step-off, plant tiers

Proposed: "Mozy Race" with crews · india · world; step-off for 1v1; leagues seed → sprout → sapling → bloom → grove. Your call; they are easy to change before launch.

D13 · store age rating

Stay out of "social media"

Always-on contests make the app 13+, which is fine. Recommended: avoid the "social media" answer (13+, 16+ in Australia, and a Social Media label) by building no feed, no browsing of strangers' content, and reactions only inside invite-only crews. Answer Apple's questionnaire again in the release that adds the race.

D14 · the privacy promise

Rewrite "No account, ever"

Proposed: "No sign-up, ever. If you join the race, only your race name and one step total per day leave your phone. Delete it any time." The App Store label, Play Data safety form, the website privacy page and the in-app Trust screen all change together.

D15 · new phones

A "Mozy ID" recovery code?

Recommended: yes, shown in the race profile, so someone who changes phone keeps their crews and history. No email needed.

D16 · league scoring

Cap what counts in leagues

Recommended: leagues count steps up to 20,000 a day, so an ultra-walker can't run away with every league and there's less reason to fake huge days. Crew, country and world boards show plain steps.

D17 · family fairness

Score families by % of their own goal?

Recommended for phase 2, as an option per crew: each person scores the share of their own daily goal (Apple and Fitbit do this), so a grandmother can beat a college student. It needs each member's daily goal to leave the phone, one number beyond your name-and-steps rule.

D18 · where the backend lives

The website repo, as its own Worker

Recommended: a race-api/ folder in the website repo, deployed separately as mozy-race. Both are Cloudflare and TypeScript, the web pages are its first users, and you keep two repos. Alternatives: a new repo, or the app repo.

D19 · API address

api.mozy.mcrudra.com

Recommended: its own hostname, so a website deploy can never break the API and routes never clash. Alternative: mozy.mcrudra.com/api through the site Worker.
